Yes. The curriculum should not be a conventional "robotics course" with a few AI electives. Based on the architecture we developed, it should be a continuous engineering, science, computation, research, innovation, manufacturing and systems curriculum in which the same conceptual substrate grows from preschool exploration into postdoctoral research.

The central educational principle should be:

> One knowledge graph, increasing depth. One engineering lifecycle, increasing autonomy. One laboratory ecosystem, increasing capability.



And by middle school, invention and intellectual property become normal outputs. By secondary school, research becomes normal. By Class 12, a strong student should be capable of producing a genuinely defensible research contribution suitable for submission to a serious journal or conference, rather than merely completing a school project.

The progression should be:

2. Natural sciences
3. Computation

6. Manufacturing and materials
7. Research methodology

A tenth domain should cut across all nine:

10. Systems thinking

Students should not simply "finish subjects".

Every learner develops a portfolio across six dimensions:

Dimension	Question

Knowledge	What do I understand?
Skill	What can I do?
Construction	What can I build?
Research	What can I discover?
Innovation	What can I invent?
Leadership	What can I define for others?


The levels become:

L0 Exposure
L1 Understanding
L2 Guided practice
L3 Independent construction
L4 Independent research
L5 Original invention
L6 Systems leadership
L7 Field-defining contribution

This prevents a PhD from becoming merely "more coursework".

58. Final educational progression

The entire programme can be summarized as:

Stage	Identity	Primary output

Preschool	Explorer	Observation
Primary	Builder	Prototype
Middle	Inventor	Invention/IP
Secondary	Engineer	Working system
Class 11	Researcher	Research study
Class 12	Young scientist/engineer	Publication + invention
Undergraduate	Systems engineer	Integrated system
Masters	Specialist	Original research
PhD	Scientist/architect	New knowledge
Postdoc	Research architect	New research field


And the deeper progression is:

Curiosity
→ Knowledge
→ Skill
→ Construction
→ Measurement
→ Experiment
→ Invention
→ Publication
→ Engineering
→ Commercialization
→ Research
→ New Knowledge
→ New Infrastructure
→ New Field

That is the curriculum I would use as the educational layer of the platform.

The important point is that preschool through postdoc should not be treated as twelve unrelated educational products. It should be one continuous mastery system in which the learner's projects, inventions, experiments, publications, patents, code, hardware, datasets, simulations, research questions and eventual research programme remain part of a persistent portfolio.

The platform therefore becomes not just a place where students learn about engineering, but a system in which a child can start with a cardboard mechanism and, fifteen years later, trace that same intellectual trajectory into a patented technology, peer-reviewed research, a manufactured system, or a new scientific/engineering field.
