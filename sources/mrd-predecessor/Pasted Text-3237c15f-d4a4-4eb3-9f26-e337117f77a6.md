Yes. This should be added as a fourth layer around the learner curriculum, alongside the student syllabus, laboratory system, and project/workbench system.

The key design principle should be:

> Do not replace the regular school/college curriculum. Map, enrich, and extend it.



A student should be able to learn the prescribed mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, computer science and STEAM curriculum while simultaneously using those concepts in engineering, research, invention and physical-AI projects.

The resulting educational promise

The platform can now support a complete loop:

> Map the formal curriculum → identify the underlying concept → teach it → demonstrate it → build something → measure it → research it → invent from it → publish it → patent or openly release it → specialize → continue into university research.



That is the important distinction.

We are not creating a separate STEAM curriculum sitting beside school education. We are creating a curriculum intelligence layer that connects ordinary education to engineering, research and innovation.

And the AI layer becomes a planning and adaptation engine for teachers, students, parents and administrators, while the canonical curriculum/knowledge graph remains the source of truth.
