# Classroom, laboratory, home and independent-learning operating model

## Teacher-led school classroom

1. Teacher selects the local curriculum edition and the course's accepted learning outcomes.
2. Teacher chooses a sequence from the prerequisite graph and sees exact/partial/unmapped coverage separately.
3. Teacher selects an explanation mode, worked example, notebook or simulation activity.
4. Learners predict before execution, work individually or in groups, and explain the result.
5. Teacher reviews formative checks, misconceptions and submitted evidence.
6. Teacher decides whether to reteach, extend or assess. Automated analytics are advisory.

## Professor-led higher education

1. Professor attaches lecture material, derivations, datasets, computational notebooks, labs and research readings to the learning outcomes.
2. Advanced students inspect assumptions, reproduce methods, compare solvers and quantify uncertainty.
3. Assessment rubrics distinguish derivation, implementation, validation, interpretation, reproducibility and limitations.
4. Research claims require citations, method provenance and independent evidence review.

## Laboratory assistant

1. Lab assistant sees the approved session, roster, equipment list and protocol.
2. The system requires calibration, risk assessment, equipment identity and supervision information for physical work.
3. The assistant records raw data, instrument metadata and deviations.
4. The assistant submits evidence to the responsible instructor or engineering authority. A lab assistant does not automatically gain grading or engineering-verification authority.

## Parent or home supporter

1. Parent access requires explicit, current consent or a verified legal authority path.
2. Parent sees supportive progress, upcoming activities and discussion prompts only within that scope.
3. Parent may record observations but cannot alter grades, mastery or official curriculum mappings.
4. Physical activities are limited to age-appropriate, risk-assessed, supervised activities.

## Independent learner

1. Learner selects a goal and completes a private, non-clinical prior-knowledge check.
2. The prerequisite graph identifies missing concepts without forcing repetition of demonstrated mastery.
3. Learner uses explanations, worked examples, notebooks, practice and reflection at a self-selected pace.
4. Learner may export a portfolio or request mentor review.

## Late learner / returning learner

Late learner is a support mode, not an age, intelligence or diagnosis label. The learner can choose text, audio, visual, concrete, symbolic or computational explanations; use short steps; retry low-stakes checks; and re-enter at the prerequisite concept actually needed. No age inference, public deficit label or automated diagnosis is allowed.

## Classroom and LMS state separation

- Curriculum edition and mapping are controlled reference data.
- Course and lesson delivery is institutional workflow.
- Notebook execution is an auditable computational activity.
- Submission and assessment are learner-specific records.
- Accepted mastery requires subject-bound evidence and an authorized reviewer.
- Course completion is not accreditation, professional qualification or engineering verification.
