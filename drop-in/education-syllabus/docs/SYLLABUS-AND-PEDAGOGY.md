# Syllabus architecture, progression and teaching design

## 1. One concept graph, many educational projections

The platform should maintain one versioned concept/prerequisite graph and project it into age/stage, qualification, subject, board, university, vocational and self-directed pathways. Curriculum standards attach as source-backed correlations to concepts; they do not define the PAP ontology.

A concept record should include:
- stable concept ID and aliases;
- prior concepts/prerequisites;
- learning outcome (observable action + context + quality criterion);
- explanation at several depths;
- worked examples and counterexamples;
- common misconceptions;
- visual, tactile, verbal, symbolic and computational representations;
- activities and notebook/simulation references;
- formative checks and rubric;
- safety profile and physical supervision requirements;
- accessibility and language variants;
- provenance and review status.

## 2. Progression bands

The MRD's preschool-to-postdoc range is retained, with a separate late-learner support mode. Bands are pedagogical projections, not deterministic age labels.

| Band | Typical cognitive work | Evidence form | Example physical-AI connection |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preschool | Notice, compare, sort, wonder, describe | Observation, oral explanation, drawing | Toy wheels, balance, light/shadow, sound |
| Primary | Measure, classify, sequence, count, explain | Simple records, model, demonstration | Build a paper mechanism; compare wheel sizes |
| Middle | Relate variables, design fair tests, make and revise | Notebook, graph, prototype sketch | Measure speed; model a sensor threshold |
| Secondary | Algebraic modelling, mechanics, circuits, data analysis | Derivation, lab report, simulation critique | RC circuit, force model, sensor calibration |
| Senior secondary | Formal calculus/physics/computing, uncertainty, design | Extended investigation, exam, design review | Control loop, robot kinematics, numerical method |
| Undergraduate | Derive, implement, validate, integrate | Code, lab, design artefact, technical report | CAD/CFD/circuit/simulation toolchain modules |
| Postgraduate | Research methods, model selection, uncertainty, reproducibility | Research proposal, benchmark, publication | Compare solvers; identify model-form error |
| Doctoral | Original contribution, falsifiable claims, defensible method | Thesis, peer-reviewed output, reproducible package | Novel control/robotics/physical-AI research |
| Postdoctoral | Field-level synthesis, standards, agenda setting | New methods, datasets, standards proposals | Reproducible research infrastructure |
| Lifelong | Goal-based reskilling and transfer | Portfolio and competency evidence | Learn a new domain tool or repair workflow |
| Late learner | Rebuild prerequisites without stigma; flexible pacing | Multiple attempts, oral/practical evidence, portfolio | Revisit algebra before control systems |

Late learner is not a deficit label or age category. It means the learner's current prerequisite graph has gaps or their preferred pace/access conditions differ. No diagnosis, intelligence ranking or age inference is performed.

## 3. Syllabus skeleton by stage

This is PAP's pedagogical spine, not a claim that a board has prescribed these exact topics.

### Foundational / preschool
- Observation, classification, spatial language, patterns, quantity, shape, cause/effect in play.
- Nature walks, object sorting, shadows, sound, water flow and safe construction play.
- No high-stakes timed assessment; adult safeguarding for physical materials.

### Primary
- Number sense, measurement, geometry, patterns, data tables, materials, simple machines, living systems and local environment.
- Activities: measure, draw, sort, build, narrate, compare and explain.
- Explanations start concrete, then move to pictures and symbols.

### Middle
- Ratios, proportionality, algebra, geometry, statistics, forces, energy, circuits, waves, computational thinking and design cycle.
- Activities: controlled experiments, spreadsheets/notebooks, graph interpretation, simulation parameter sweeps, simple safe prototypes.
- Introduce model assumptions, repeatability and error.

### Secondary / senior secondary
- Functions, calculus foundations, vectors, probability, mechanics, electricity/magnetism, chemistry, biology, computing, algorithms, systems and engineering design.
- Activities: derivations, lab notebooks, numerical experiments, circuit analysis, CAD concepts, simulation validation, data analysis.
- Explicitly distinguish mathematical model, numerical result, measurement and inference.

### Undergraduate
- Linear algebra, calculus, differential equations, numerical methods, mechanics, thermodynamics, fluid mechanics, materials, electronics, signals, control, algorithms, software architecture, systems engineering, safety and verification.
- Projects: reproducible simulation, CAD/CAE integration, circuit design, embedded control, requirements traceability, test evidence.

### Postgraduate / doctoral / postdoctoral
- Advanced modelling, uncertainty quantification, formal methods, research design, scientific computing, optimization, multi-physics, robotics, manufacturing systems, standards, reproducibility, peer review and open research infrastructure.
- Assess assumptions, proof obligations, numerical convergence, independent validation, novelty and limitations.

## 4. Explanations and worked examples

Every concept should support a ladder of explanations:
1. Intuitive: everyday analogy, carefully bounded.
2. Concrete: objects, measurement or experiment.
3. Visual: diagram, graph, geometry or simulation.
4. Symbolic: definitions, equations and derivation.
5. Computational: executable notebook, model or code.
6. Critical: assumptions, counterexamples, uncertainty and failure modes.
7. Transfer: new domain or real-world problem.

A good explanation states what is being explained, what assumptions hold, why the result follows, where it fails and how a learner can test it. Do not use analogy as proof.

## 5. Teaching modes by role

- Teacher: lesson planning, pacing, classroom discussion, differentiation, formative checks and standards coverage.
- Professor: derivations, research literature, model critique, advanced notebooks and open-ended assessment.
- Lab assistant: equipment readiness, calibration, risk assessment, supervised procedure, raw-data capture and deviations.
- Parent: supportive questions, home observations and progress visibility; no automatic grading or diagnosis.
- Student: prediction, exploration, explanation, practice, reflection and portfolio.
- Independent learner: self-paced pathway, prerequisite repair, notebook execution, rubric self-check and next-step planning.
- Administrator: access, safeguarding, curriculum edition, aggregate coverage and quality review.
- Curriculum reviewer: source verification, mapping review, versioning and change impact.

## 6. Late learners and accessibility

Support should be based on demonstrated prerequisite needs and learner-selected accommodations, not age stereotypes. Features include short steps, plain-language explanations, glossary, audio/text alternatives, worked examples, extra practice, alternate representations, self-paced retries, low-bandwidth mode, printable/exportable notes, language/script choices, keyboard navigation, captions, screen-reader structure and high-contrast layouts. A learner may skip content already mastered when evidence supports it, while retaining the pathway and rationale.

Avoid public leaderboards, shame-based progress indicators, opaque learner risk scores and sensitive inference. Parent/teacher dashboards must expose only authorized learner data.
